# Cinematic Language

## Documentary cinematic baseline

Favor observational, geographically clear shots that feel captured rather than staged.

## Shot vocabulary

- `aerial_establishing`: establish biome and actor geography.
- `wide_observational`: show behavior with environmental context.
- `medium_tracking`: follow a moving subject while retaining context.
- `long_lens_observation`: compressed documentary view from apparent distance.
- `low_threat_reveal`: low camera for predator reveal; use sparingly.
- `close_detail`: eye, claw, breath, rain, wound or foliage detail.
- `reaction`: readable subject reaction to a new threat/event.
- `static_hide`: camera partially obscured by vegetation, documentary feel.

## Continuity rules

- Establish geography before complex action.
- Preserve screen direction during pursuit unless a deliberate reset shot is inserted.
- Avoid arbitrary lens changes inside a continuous beat.
- Do not cross the action axis repeatedly during fights.
- Use cutaways to reset geography when needed.

## Camera movement

Prefer:
- subtle handheld/sway for observational shots
- dolly/tracking for pursuit
- crane/aerial for establishing geography
- static telephoto for animal behavior

Avoid constant orbiting and game-like chase-camera framing in documentary sequences.

## Editing rhythm

- Establishing: 5–10 s
- Behavior observation: 4–10 s
- Suspense/stalking: 3–7 s
- Fast combat beats: 2–5 s
- Reaction / aftermath: 3–8 s

## Lighting

Use environmental motivation first: sun, overcast sky, storm, practical park lights, fire, moonlight. Cinematic fill/rim lighting may be added, but should not make the scene feel like a stage unless explicitly requested.
