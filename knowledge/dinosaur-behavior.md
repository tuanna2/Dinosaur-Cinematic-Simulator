# Dinosaur Behavior Guidance

This document is production guidance for believable cinematic behavior, not a claim of exact paleobiological reconstruction.

## General principles

- Dinosaurs should behave as animals, not human combatants.
- Most encounters should include detection, assessment, signaling and avoidance before direct combat.
- Movement should respect body mass and turning radius.
- Large animals should not stop, rotate or accelerate instantly.
- Predators should not attack continuously without recovery or repositioning.
- Herd/prey animals should prioritize escape, defensive orientation and group spacing.

## Predator sequence vocabulary

Typical progression:
1. idle / patrol
2. detect
3. orient
4. stalk / approach
5. threaten / vocalize
6. chase
7. attack attempt
8. recover / reposition
9. continue or disengage

## Prey / defensive sequence vocabulary

Typical progression:
1. graze / drink / travel
2. notice
3. orient toward threat
4. warning posture
5. flee or defend
6. charge / horn / tail defense when applicable
7. recover / escape

## Pack behavior

For pack scenes:
- avoid perfect synchronized movement
- assign lead/flank/rear roles
- maintain spatial separation
- stagger attacks
- allow individuals to retreat after a failed attack

## T-Rex cinematic baseline

- Heavy locomotion and visible weight transfer.
- Short bursts of aggression rather than constant sprinting.
- Threat display and roar may precede attack for cinematic purposes.

## Velociraptor cinematic baseline

- Use coordinated but imperfect group behavior.
- Favor flanking, probing and short attack windows.
- Avoid eight animals attacking the same exact point simultaneously.

## Triceratops cinematic baseline

- Orient horns toward perceived threats.
- Prefer defensive stance when cornered.
- Can break into a committed run toward open terrain.
- Turning and braking should communicate mass.
